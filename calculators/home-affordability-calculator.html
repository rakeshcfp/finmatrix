💡 Smart Money Breakdown: Key Concepts Explained in Plain English

📏 The 28/36 Underwriting Rule

Standard mortgage lending guideline: spend no more than 28% of gross income on housing costs, and no more than 36% on total debt obligations.

📊 Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio

The percentage of gross monthly income allocated toward recurring debt payments. Lenders require low DTI to approve prime mortgage interest rates.

📐 The 28/36 DTI Underwriting Equations

Max PITI = Gross Monthly Income * 0.28

Max Total Debts = Gross Monthly Income * 0.36

Worked Example: A household earning $110,000/yr ($9,166/mo) with $400 in monthly car debt qualifies for a maximum monthly PITI payment of $2,566, supporting a maximum home purchase price of approximately $415,000 with 10% down.

28/36 Debt-to-Income (DTI) Underwriting Standards

Conforming Lending Standards: Conventional mortgage underwriters follow the 28/36 DTI guideline to assess borrower debt service safety and ensure loan qualification.

Front-End DTI (≤ 28%): Total monthly housing expense (PITI + PMI + HOA) must not exceed 28% of gross monthly income.
Back-End DTI (≤ 36%–43%): Total monthly recurring debt service (housing + car + student debt + credit cards) must not exceed 36% (or 43% Qualified Mortgage cap).

Regulatory Grounding: Fannie Mae Selling Guide; CFPB Qualified Mortgage Standards (12 CFR Part 1026).

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the 28/36 rule for home affordability?

The 28/36 rule dictates that housing expenses (PITI) should not exceed 28% of gross monthly income, and total debt obligations should not exceed 36%.

Q: How does Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio affect mortgage approval?

Lenders evaluate DTI to measure repayment capacity. Lower DTI ratios qualify borrowers for higher loan amounts and competitive interest rates.
