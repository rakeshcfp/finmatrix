How This Is Calculated: Tax Arbitrage Math

The choice between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA represents a mathematical arbitrage between your marginal tax rate today and your effective tax rate in retirement:

Traditional IRA Wealth: [ C × (1 + r)ⁿ ] × (1 − t_retirement) + [ Invested Tax Savings ]

Roth IRA Wealth: [ C × (1 + r)ⁿ ] (100% Tax-Free)

The Fundamental Rule: If your tax rate today is higher than in retirement (e.g. 24% today vs 12% in retirement), a Traditional IRA wins—provided you invest the upfront annual tax refund ($1,800/yr). If your tax rate today is lower than in retirement (e.g. young professionals in the 10% or 12% bracket expecting peak career wealth), a Roth IRA delivers massive, unassailable tax-free gains.

Assumptions & Official Sources

  • Statutory Contribution Limits: Adheres to IRS Notice 2025-xx setting the 2026 annual IRA contribution limit at $7,500 for individuals under age 50, and $8,600 ($1,100 catch-up) for age 50+.
  • Tax Savings Reinvestment: Assumes the upfront tax deduction generated by Traditional IRA contributions is invested in a taxable brokerage account compounding at the same market return rate (with a 15% annual tax drag on dividends/gains).
  • Qualified Withdrawals: Assumes all Roth withdrawals satisfy the statutory 5-year aging rule and occur after age 59½, eliminating the 10% early withdrawal penalty.
  • Official Citations: IRS Publication 590-A (Contributions to Individual Retirement Arrangements) (IRS.gov/p590a) and IRS Publication 590-B (Distributions from Individual Retirement Arrangements).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the primary difference between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA?
Traditional IRAs provide tax deductions today, but your withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income. Roth IRAs are funded with after-tax dollars today, but all investment growth and retirement withdrawals after age 59½ are completely tax-free.
What is the 2026 IRA contribution limit?
The 2026 IRA contribution limit is $7,500 for savers under age 50. For individuals age 50 and older, the limit is $8,600 ($7,500 base plus $1,100 catch-up).
What are the 2026 Roth IRA income phaseout limits?
For 2026, the modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) phaseout range for direct Roth contributions is $153,000 to $168,000 for single filers, and $240,000 to $250,000 for married couples filing jointly. Above these thresholds, taxpayers frequently use the Backdoor Roth IRA strategy.
Can I contribute to both a 401(k) and an IRA in 2026?
Yes! In 2026, you can max out both your employer 401(k) ($24,500 limit) and your personal IRA ($7,500 limit), providing up to $32,000 in combined annual tax-advantaged retirement contributions.
