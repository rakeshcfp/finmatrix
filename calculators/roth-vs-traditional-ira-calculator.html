💡 Smart Money Breakdown: Key Concepts Explained in Plain English

✨ Roth IRA (Tax-Free Growth)

Contributions are made with post-tax dollars. All investment growth and future qualified withdrawals in retirement are 100% tax-free.

🏦 Traditional IRA (Upfront Tax Break)

Contributions may be tax-deductible today, lowering current taxable income, while withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.

📐 Tax-Adjusted Future Value Equations

Roth FV = Contribution * (1+r)^n (Tax-Free Withdrawals)

Traditional FV = Contribution * (1+r)^n * (1 - TaxRate_Retirement)

Worked Example: Maxing out a Roth IRA at $7,000/yr for 30 years at 8% growth yields $856,300 in 100% tax-free capital, saving ~$188,000 in retirement taxes compared to a Traditional IRA in a 22% tax bracket.

🎯 How to Choose Between Roth and Traditional IRA

  1. Choose Roth if Current Tax Bracket is Low: Early career workers in lower tax brackets (12% or 22%) benefit most from locking in tax-free growth.
  2. Choose Traditional if Current Tax Bracket is Peak: High earners in top brackets (32%+) gain immediate relief through upfront tax deductions.
  3. Use the Backdoor Roth IRA Option: High earners exceeding IRS income caps can make non-deductible Traditional IRA contributions and convert them to Roth tax-free.

2026 IRA Contribution Limits & Marginal Tax Bracket Arbitrage

The Arbitrage Theorem: The decision between a Roth and Traditional IRA depends on the spread between your current marginal tax bracket today and your projected effective tax bracket in retirement.

Traditional Advantage: Optimal when current marginal tax rate (e.g., 24%–35%) exceeds expected retirement effective tax rate.
Roth Advantage: Optimal in early career (10%–12% brackets) or for investors seeking tax-free growth and zero mandatory RMDs.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the primary tax difference between a Roth IRA and Traditional IRA?

Roth IRAs use post-tax dollars for tax-free growth and tax-free retirement withdrawals. Traditional IRAs offer upfront tax deductions but withdrawals are taxed.

Q: What are the IRA contribution limits for 2025/2026?

The annual IRS IRA contribution limit is $7,000 for individuals under age 50, with an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution ($8,000 total) for ages 50+.
