How Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratios Are Calculated

Mortgage underwriters evaluate two distinct mathematical metrics to determine loan affordability:

Front-End DTI (Housing Ratio): [ Monthly Proposed Housing Cost ÷ Gross Monthly Income ] × 100

Back-End DTI (Total Debt Ratio): [ (Housing + Auto + Student Loans + Credit Minimums) ÷ Gross Monthly Income ] × 100

The 28/36 Rule of Thumb: Financial institutions traditionally prefer a front-end ratio of 28% or less and a back-end ratio of 36% or less. In high-cost housing markets, conforming conventional loans frequently permit back-end DTIs up to 43% (and up to 45%–50% with automated underwriting approval).

Worked Example: If gross monthly household income is $8,000 with an $1,800 mortgage PITI, a $350 auto loan, a $200 student loan, and $100 in credit card minimums, total monthly debt obligations equal $2,450:

Front-End DTI = $1,800 ÷ $8,000 = 22.5% (Well under 28% limit)
Back-End DTI = $2,450 ÷ $8,000 = 30.6% (Well under 36% conventional benchmark)

Assumptions & Official Sources

  • Gross Income Standards: Uses gross pre-tax income prior to federal, state, and payroll deductions, consistent with Fannie Mae Single-Family Selling Guide B3-6-02.
  • Debt Definitions: Excludes non-debt living expenses (food, cell phone, car insurance, streaming services) in accordance with CFPB guidelines.
  • Authoritative Citations: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau QM DTI Benchmark (CFPB.gov) and Fannie Mae Eligibility Matrix (FannieMae.com).

* Disclaimer: Mortgage approval is subject to credit score (FICO), loan-to-value (LTV) ratio, employment history, and asset verification. This calculator is provided for estimation purposes only.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good DTI ratio to buy a house?
A back-end DTI of 36% or lower is considered excellent for conventional mortgage qualification. A DTI between 36% and 43% is acceptable for many conforming and FHA loans, while DTIs above 43% require strong credit scores and cash reserves.
What debts are included in DTI?
DTI includes recurring contractual debt: mortgage payments, auto loans, student loans, credit card minimums, and personal loans. It does not include utilities, groceries, or car insurance.
How can I quickly improve my DTI ratio?
Pay off small credit card balances completely to eliminate their monthly minimum payments, or refinance existing loans to lower monthly installments.
