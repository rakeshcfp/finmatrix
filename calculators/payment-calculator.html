How Monthly Loan Payments Are Calculated

Every standard fixed-rate loan utilizes the periodic amortization equation. This mathematical model determines the exact monthly installment required so that after n periods, the principal balance reaches zero while accounting for compound interest charges accrued each billing cycle:

M = P × [ r(1 + r)n ] / [ (1 + r)n - 1 ]

Where P represents the principal borrowed, r represents the monthly interest rate (Annual APR divided by 12), and n is the total number of monthly payments.

Frequently Asked Questions (PAA)

How does extra monthly principal save me money? +

Every extra dollar paid goes 100% to principal reduction. Since interest is calculated based on your remaining balance, prepayments immediately reduce compound interest charges and shave months or years off your debt horizon.

What is the difference between APR and interest rate? +

The interest rate is the base cost of borrowing the principal balance annually. The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) reflects the true annual cost including lender fees, origination charges, and points.
