How 401(k) Compound Growth & Matching Work

A 401(k) combines two of the most potent wealth multipliers in personal finance: immediate employer matching (an instant 50% to 100% return) and tax-deferred compounding:

1. Employee Deferral: min(Salary × Contribution %, $24,500 statutory 2026 cap)
2. Employer Match: Salary × min(Contribution %, Match Cap %) × Match Ratio
3. Annual Compound Engine: Ending Balance = (Starting Balance + Total Annual Additions) × (1 + Return %)

The "Free Money" Rule: Financial advisors universally recommend contributing at least enough to capture your full employer match. For example, if your employer offers a 50% match up to 6% of salary, contributing 6% guarantees an immediate 50% risk-free return before your money even enters the stock market.

Worked Example: On a $100,000 salary with a 10% contribution ($10,000/yr) and a 50% match up to 6% ($3,000/yr), you invest $13,000 annually. Over 25 years with 3% annual salary raises and a 7.0% return on an initial $50,000 balance, your personal out-of-pocket contributions total $364,593. Meanwhile, your employer contributes $109,378, and compound interest produces $824,249, yielding a final nest egg of $1,348,220!

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 2026 401(k) contribution limit?
The 2026 employee elective deferral limit is $24,500. Savers aged 50 and older can contribute an extra $8,000 catch-up contribution, for a total of $32,500.
How does an employer match work?
An employer match is when your employer deposits money into your 401(k) based on your contributions, such as 50 cents per dollar up to 6% of your salary.
Does company match count toward the $24,500 limit?
No. The $24,500 limit applies only to employee salary deferrals. Total additions (employee + employer) can reach up to $72,000 in 2026.
