How Extra Payments Crush Student Loan Interest

Student loan interest accrues daily using the simple daily interest formula: Daily Interest = (Unpaid Principal × Interest Rate) ÷ 365.25.

When you make only your standard minimum payment, a significant portion goes to accrued interest. But when you make an extra monthly payment applied directly to principal, you immediately reduce the balance on which future daily interest is calculated. This creates a compounding effect in reverse: every extra dollar reduces next month's interest, forcing even more of your regular monthly payment toward principal.

Worked Example: On a $30,000 balance at 6.0% APR over 10 years, your standard monthly payment is $333.06, totaling $9,967 in interest. Adding just $100 extra per month increases your payment to $433.06. This wipes out the debt in 89 months (7.4 years) instead of 120 months, eliminating 31 months of payments and saving $2,763 in pure interest!

Assumptions & Official Sources

  • Principal Allocation: Assumes the borrower instructs their servicer (e.g. MOHELA, Nelnet, Aidvantage) to apply excess payments directly to principal rather than advancing the due date.
  • Fixed-Rate Loans: Modeled with standard fixed-interest federal direct Stafford/PLUS loans or private fixed-rate student loans.
  • Official Federal Sources: Repayment plan guidelines per Federal Student Aid (StudentAid.gov) and Consumer Financial Protection Bureau Higher Education tools.

* Disclaimer: This calculator does not model Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) forgiveness or Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF). If you are pursuing forgiveness, making extra payments may reduce your overall forgiveness benefit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do extra payments automatically reduce principal?
Federal rules require payments to cover any outstanding daily accrued interest first. Ensure your servicer applies the extra balance directly to principal instead of putting your account into 'paid ahead status'.
Can I deduct student loan interest on taxes?
Yes, eligible taxpayers can deduct up to $2,500 in student loan interest paid per year on IRS Form 1040 without needing to itemize deductions.
Should I make extra payments if I qualify for PSLF?
No! If you are on track for Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF), making extra payments will only pay off debt that would otherwise be forgiven tax-free after 120 qualifying monthly payments.
