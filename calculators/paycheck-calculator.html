📐 How This Is Calculated

Net take-home pay is computed by deducting statutory Federal Income Tax brackets, Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) payroll taxes, and applicable state payroll withholdings from gross salary, after subtracting eligible pre-tax elective deferrals:

Net Pay = Gross Pay - PreTax Deferrals - Federal Income Tax - FICA (7.65%) - State Tax

• Pre-Tax Deferrals: Traditional 401(k) contributions (up to $24,500 in 2026) and HSA/health insurance premiums reduce gross income dollar-for-dollar before income taxes are computed.
• Social Security (OASDI): 6.20% withholding is applied on gross earnings up to the 2026 statutory wage cap of $184,500. Earnings above this limit incur 0% OASDI tax.
• Medicare (HI): 1.45% is levied uncapped across all earnings, plus an additional 0.90% surtax on income exceeding $200,000 for single filers.
• Worked Example: An annual gross salary of $85,000 paid biweekly (26 pay periods) yields $3,269.23 in gross wages per paycheck. With a 5% 401(k) contribution ($163.46) and $69.23 health premium, taxable wages equal $3,036.54. Deducting $296 in Federal withholding, $250 in FICA, and $145 in state tax yields a final net take-home pay of $2,414 per bi-weekly paycheck.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 2026 Social Security FICA wage cap?

For Tax Year 2026, the Social Security (OASDI) maximum taxable earnings limit is $184,500. Earnings above this amount are not subject to the 6.2% Social Security payroll tax, capping an employee's maximum annual contribution at $11,439.00.

What are the 2026 Standard Deduction amounts?

Under IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 for Tax Year 2026: Single and Married Filing Separately: $16,100; Married Filing Jointly: $32,200; Head of Household: $24,150.

How does increasing my 401(k) contribution affect take-home pay?

Because 401(k) contributions are deducted pre-tax, contributing $100 to your 401(k) does NOT reduce your take-home pay by $100. If you are in the 22% federal bracket and a 5% state tax bracket, each $100 contribution only reduces your net paycheck by approximately $73, saving you $27 in immediate taxes.

Which states have zero income tax on paychecks?

Residents of Texas, Florida, Washington, Nevada, Tennessee, Wyoming, South Dakota, Alaska, and New Hampshire pay 0% state income tax on wage income, maximizing net take-home pay compared to high-tax states.
