How This Is Calculated: The Unrecoverable Cost Framework

Traditional rent vs. buy comparisons commit a critical error: they simply contrast a monthly mortgage payment against monthly rent, ignoring the massive unrecoverable costs of homeownership and the opportunity cost of tied-up down payment equity.

Unrecoverable Buying Costs: Mortgage Interest + Property Taxes + Maintenance (1%) + Home Insurance + 6% Realtor Selling Fees

Unrecoverable Renting Costs: Monthly Rent Payments

Opportunity Cost: Down payment & transaction fees compounded in the stock market (S&P 500) over the same horizon

Worked Example: On a $400,000 home with 20% down ($80,000) and $12,000 in closing costs, the buyer commits $92,000 in cash upfront. Over a 10-year horizon with 3% home appreciation, the property value increases to $537,567. Meanwhile, the loan principal reduces to $257,000. After subtracting a standard 6% real estate broker selling fee ($32,254), the buyer's net cash equity is $248,313.

Conversely, the renter invests that same initial $92,000 into a balanced index fund portfolio achieving 7% annualized return, growing to $180,967. Adding the monthly difference between owner housing outlays and rent gives the renter a total accumulated net worth of $194,110. In this specific scenario, buying delivers a net advantage of $54,203.

Assumptions & Official Sources

  • Maintenance Rule: Home maintenance is modeled at 1.0% of the home's market value annually, covering appliance replacement, roof maintenance, and structural upkeep.
  • Transaction Friction: Buying closing costs are modeled at 3.0% of purchase price; selling transaction costs (agent commissions, transfer taxes) are modeled at 6.0% upon disposition.
  • Reinvestment Discipline: Assumes the renter diligently invests 100% of the down payment savings and any monthly cash savings into low-cost index funds compounding monthly.
  • Data Sources: Benchmark metrics derived from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED) Housing Data (FRED) and U.S. Census Bureau Home Financing Surveys (Census.gov).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 5% rule in the rent vs buy decision?
The 5% rule breaks down unrecoverable ownership costs into three parts: 1% for municipal property taxes, 1% for annual home maintenance/repairs, and 3% for cost of capital (mortgage interest or lost investment returns). Multiplying the home price by 5% and dividing by 12 yields the monthly rent threshold. If equivalent rent is below that number, renting is mathematically superior.
Is renting really 'throwing money away'?
No. Renting pays for shelter, eliminates maintenance liabilities, and preserves mobility. Furthermore, in the early years of a 30-year mortgage, up to 75% of your monthly mortgage payment goes to interest, taxes, and insurance rather than building equity.
How does investing the down payment capital change the outcome?
Down payments ($80,000 or more) represent significant capital that is tied up in a single physical asset. If invested in the stock market (e.g. S&P 500 historical ~7% real return), compound interest can outpace real estate appreciation in many geographic markets.
What is the typical break-even timeline for buying a house?
Because of 2%–5% purchase closing costs and 6% seller realtor commissions, homeownership typically requires a minimum stay of 4 to 7 years to break even against renting.
