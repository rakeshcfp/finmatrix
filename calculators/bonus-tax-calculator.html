How Bonus Taxes Work: Withholding vs. Actual Tax

When workers receive a bonus, they are often shocked to see 30% to 45% missing from their check. This occurs because the IRS requires employers to treat bonuses, commissions, severance, and overtime as supplemental wages governed by IRS Publication 15-T:

1. Federal Supplemental Rate: Exactly 22% flat withholding (or 37% for amounts exceeding $1,000,000)
2. Social Security: 6.2% up to the 2026 cap of $184,500 across combined salary + bonus
3. Medicare: 1.45% uncapped (+ 0.9% additional over $200k single / $250k married)
4. State Withholding: Fixed supplemental rate (e.g., CA 10.23%, NY 11.70%, IL 4.95%)

The Key Difference: Withholding ≠ Final Tax: Withholding is merely a preliminary prepayment toward your annual tax liability. At tax season, your bonus is lumped together with your regular salary on Form 1040. If your salary puts you in the 12% marginal bracket, you overpaid by 10% on your bonus and will receive a cash tax refund. If your salary puts you in the 24% or 32% bracket, you underpaid by 2% to 10% and will owe the difference!

Assumptions & Official Sources

  • Publication 15-T: Adheres to official IRS Publication 15-T Section 7 (Supplemental Wages) flat 22% percentage method (IRS.gov/p15t).
  • FICA Cap: Social Security tax is calculated factoring your regular YTD salary against the verified 2026 cap of $184,500. If your base salary already exceeds $184,500, 0% Social Security tax is withheld from your bonus.
  • Aggregate Method Alternative: Employers occasionally use the "aggregate method" (taxing the bonus as if it were regular pay spread across pay periods). This calculator models the standard flat percentage method utilized by over 90% of US corporate payroll processors.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are bonuses taxed at such a high rate?
Bonuses are not taxed at a higher rate; they are withheld at a statutory flat 22% federal rate, plus 7.65% FICA and state payroll taxes. This upfront withholding prevents workers from owing large tax penalties when filing their annual return.
Do I get money back from my bonus taxes when I file?
Yes, if your final taxable income falls in the 10% or 12% federal brackets, you will receive a refund for the excess tax withheld from your bonus.
Can I defer my bonus into my 401(k) to save on taxes?
Yes, if your company payroll system supports it, you can elect to contribute up to 100% of your bonus into a pre-tax 401(k) (subject to the 2026 $24,500 annual limit), avoiding both the 22% federal withholding and state income taxes.
