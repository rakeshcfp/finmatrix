How This Is Calculated: The Liquidity Trade-Off

Both High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSAs) and Certificates of Deposit (CDs) calculate interest using monthly compounding:

A = P × (1 + r ÷ 12)ⁿ

Where P is deposit principal, r is the annual APY rate, and n is the number of months held.

The Early Withdrawal Penalty Risk: If you withdraw money from a CD prior to maturity, the financial institution imposes a penalty (typically 90 days of interest on a 1-year CD). On a $25,000 deposit at 5.10% APY, a 3-month penalty equals $318.75. If you break the CD at month 6, your net earned interest drops from $637 to $319, which is significantly worse than the $531 you would have earned in a penalty-free HYSA!

Assumptions & Official Sources

  • Compounding Standard: Modeled with standard monthly interest compounding.
  • FDIC Insurance: Both account types assume deposit at an FDIC-insured or NCUA-insured institution up to the federal $250,000 limit (FDIC.gov).
  • Variable HYSA Assumptions: Models HYSA rate as constant over the comparison timeframe; in real-world conditions, HYSA rates fluctuate following Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) benchmark federal funds rate adjustments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an HYSA and a CD?
An HYSA offers flexible liquidity with variable interest rates that can change at any time. A CD locks in a fixed rate for a specific term, but charges penalties for withdrawing funds early.
What is a CD ladder strategy?
A CD ladder splits your cash across multiple CDs with staggered maturity dates (e.g., 3-month, 6-month, 9-month, and 12-month terms). This ensures regular liquidity while capturing higher long-term yields.
Is interest on HYSAs and CDs taxable?
Yes. Interest earned on both HYSAs and CDs is taxable as ordinary income in the year it is credited and reported on IRS Form 1099-INT.
