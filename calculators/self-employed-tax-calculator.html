How Self-Employment Taxes Are Calculated (2026 Rules)

Unlike corporate employees who split payroll taxes with an employer, self-employed taxpayers (sole proprietors, independent contractors, freelancers, and single-member LLCs) are subject to the Self-Employment Contributions Act (SECA) tax rate of 15.3%:

1. Net Schedule C Profit = Gross 1099 Revenue − Business Expenses
2. Taxable SECA Base = Net Profit × 0.9235 (IRC § 1402(a)(12))
3. Social Security (12.4%) = Capped at statutory $184,500 wage base (2026 limit)
4. Medicare (2.9%) = Uncapped on all self-employment earnings (+ 0.9% > $200k)
5. 50% Above-the-Line SECA Deduction = Half of your SECA tax reduces federal taxable income

The 20% QBI Pass-Through Deduction: Under IRC Section 199A, most small business owners can deduct up to 20% of their net business profit from federal taxable income, dramatically reducing effective marginal tax rates.

Worked Example: If you bill $100,000 with $15,000 in Schedule C deductions, your net profit is $85,000. Your SECA taxable base is $78,497.50 ($85,000 × 0.9235), resulting in $12,010 in SECA taxes ($9,734 Social Security + $2,276 Medicare). You then deduct half of that ($6,005) plus the 20% QBI deduction ($17,000) and your 2026 Single Standard Deduction ($16,100), leaving just $45,895 in federal taxable income!

Assumptions & Official Sources

  • FICA Cap: Social Security tax (12.4%) applies up to the verified 2026 statutory wage base of $184,500. Medicare (2.9%) is uncapped.
  • Standard Deductions (2026): Single filers receive $16,100; Married Filing Jointly receives $32,200; Head of Household receives $24,150 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32).
  • QBI Qualification: Assumes pass-through business profit qualifies for the full 20% Section 199A deduction under applicable taxable income thresholds.
  • Official IRS Citations: IRS Schedule C (IRS.gov/schedule-c), Schedule SE (IRS.gov/schedule-se), and Form 1040-ES Estimated Tax Instructions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is self-employment tax 15.3% instead of 7.65%?
On a W-2, your employer covers half of your FICA tax (7.65%), and you pay the other half. When self-employed, you act as both employer and employee, making you responsible for the entire 15.3% SECA total.
What is the 92.35% multiplier for self-employment tax?
The IRS does not charge SECA tax on your entire profit. It first multiplies your net Schedule C earnings by 92.35% (reflecting a deduction for the employer-equivalent portion).
When are 2026 quarterly estimated tax payments due?
The IRS requires four equal estimated tax payments on: April 15, 2026 (Q1), June 15, 2026 (Q2), September 15, 2026 (Q3), and January 15, 2027 (Q4).
How does the 20% QBI deduction work?
Under IRC Section 199A, qualifying small business owners can deduct 20% of their net profit from their taxable income before calculating federal income tax, providing massive tax relief for pass-through entities.
